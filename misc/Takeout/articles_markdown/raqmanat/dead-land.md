Many people assume that earning a living must follow one path:  
  
School → University → Job → Salary.  
  
But this model is only one way people organize work. It is not the only way.  
  
In many parts of the tech world, people build something different. Instead of creating companies, they create projects.  
  
These projects are often open source. Developers from different places contribute, improve the work, and build around it.  
  
The project grows over time, and eventually it can be monetized in different ways: services, premium features, support, sponsorships, or related tools.  
  
The key difference is independence.  
Instead of entering an existing system and becoming a small piece inside it, a person builds something that belongs to them.  
  
It takes initiative and patience, because in the beginning there is no salary waiting at the end of the month. There is only tawakkul and effort.  
  
But if the work is solid and beneficial, momentum begins to build.  
People start using the project. Others contribute. Opportunities appear.  
  
What began as a small effort slowly becomes a sustainable source of income.  
  
This model already exists and is widely used. It's just that the majority are terrified of it.  
  
It simply requires a different mindset.  
It requires someone willing to think beyond the usual path and take responsibility for their own work.  
  
For Muslims, this independence can also allow more freedom to dedicate time and effort to beneficial work and daʿwah, without being tied to structures that may restrict it.  
  
In the end, every path to provision comes from the same place:

وما توفيقي إلا بالله.

To help bring this closer, imagine it as a dead land — one that looks like it can't bear crops, but it actually could, given deep insight and initiative.

Anyone who tries to work on this land might look crazy, foolish, or simply weird. But he's actually the only one who looked closely enough to see what's buried under the surface, while everyone else just glanced and walked past.

"Why work on this piece of land, and not join the rest of us in the green, lush pastures? You won't do much — it's dead. Just drop it".

That voice always sounds wise. Calm, experienced, looking out for you. But it has never tilled anything itself. What it calls wisdom is just fear, dressed carefully enough to pass for foresight.

That's the kind of narrow mindset that's disabling us. Reality is already hard enough; this kind of speech just adds a mental load on top of it.

And it's worth remembering: those standing in the green pastures didn't make the land green. Someone before them did the work of clearing it, tilling it, waiting on it — then handed it down. The pessimist inherited ease and mistook it for wisdom.

The one working the dead land isn't reckless. He sees what they can't — not because he's gifted with some secret, but because he's willing to look closer and stay longer than anyone around him thinks makes sense.

Al-Albānī lived this exact resistance. The people around him were certain his work would lead nowhere. Now people walk through fields he was the one who broke ground on — never having seen them dead, eating from a harvest they assume was always there.

—Abū Sahl, ʿAbdurraḥmān bnu Mīhūb al-Qaddārī al-Jazāʾirī
