# THE CURRENCY OF SINCERITY

— On the Decoupling of Expression and Reality —

### AL-QADDĀRĪ

― ✦ ―

بِسْمِ اللَّهِ الرَّحْمَنِ الرَّحِيم

## Introduction

Ash-shaykh Sulaymān al-Ruḥaylī noted that some people, whenever they meet someone, say "I love you for the sake of Allāh" — despite knowing of no reason that would warrant such love. He called this a mistake.

Indeed, it is a mistake.

Love for the sake of Allāh is not created by saying the words. The words are supposed to describe a reality that already exists — a reality rooted in the beloved's righteousness, his adherence to the Sunnah, his defense of the truth.

The love exists, it has real substance, and the statement merely informs the other person of that.

But if the cause disappears and the expression remains, the language drifts away from reality, which is the signature problem of our era's religious social culture.

― ✦ ―

## The Inflation of  
Religious Language

This dilution spreads across words, words that once carried immense gravity, words that suffered from over-circulation until their value faded away.

Praise is now effortless, and you can love immediately, and be a brother to anyone.

You can spend religious language freely, and not worry about the realities it was meant to represent.

Save the vocabulary only; the purchasing power is not our concern.

That leads to expressions being decoupled from their underlying substance, they cease to function as testimonies and begin to function more like decor.

This devastating effect is the consequence of a culture accustomed to immediate results, where individuals reach for the highest, most sacred language available to simulate an intimacy that has not yet been forged through trial or time.

They attempt to substitute the labor of building a true spiritual bond with the immediate gratification of declaring it.

The inevitable result of this over-printing is the opposite of the sought deep connection; a profound, unvoiced skepticism.

When the grandest terms are distributed indiscriminately, they lose their capacity to signify anything meaningful.

That in turn leads to hesitation to trust such language.

A logical response of a mind recognizing that the currency of speech has been thoroughly deflated.

― ✦ ―

## The Precision Of the Salaf

These modern sensibilities run directly counter to the methodology of the salaf.

Let’s look at, for example, the encounter between Abū Idrīs al-Khawlānī and Muʿāth bnu Jabal.

Abū Idrīs said to Muʿāth: "Wallāhi, I love you for the sake of Allāh".

Muʿāth did not return the words. Instead, he demanded: "Āllāh?"

An investigation.

Abū Idrīs answered: "Āllāh".

Muʿāth asked a second time: "Āllāh?"

Abū Idrīs repeated: "Āllāh".

Muʿāth asked a third time: "Āllāh?"

Abū Idrīs answered: "Āllāh".

Abū Idrīs offered no objection, no defensive pivot; he simply answered the great Companion with absolute submission to the gravity of the oath.

Only then did Muʿāth take hold of his garment, pull him close, and say: "Rejoice—for I heard the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ say: Allāh tabāraka wa taʿālā said: 'My love is due to those who love one another in Me, who sit together in Me, who visit one another in Me, and who give to one another in Me'".

Three times he asked. An interrogation.

Modern sensibilities detect this as emotional coldness.

But Muʿāth understood that "I love you for the sake of Allāh" is not a social greeting. It is a testimony. A testimony requires that the one giving it knows precisely what he is standing behind.

The testimony was given its right, and was not treated lightly.

The two men dealt with an immense reality. One carefully brought it forward; the other received it only after its weight was verified.

That’s the precision of the salaf. Their love had causes. Their praise had causes. Their criticism had causes.

Speech was not dictated by sentiment, but by an underlying reality they could stand behind.

We managed to reverse this successfully. We deploy the expression, then pretend its substance follows through automatically. Then, we act surprised when real circumstances expose this naivety.

The fragmentation of contemporary social ties ceases to be a mystery, then.

― ✦ ―

## Verbal Softness

This reversal creates an obvious problem.

When language no longer guarantees reality, trust breaks down. Yet people still need a way to judge who is safe and who is virtuous.

They need a shortcut. People love shortcuts.

To fill that gap, we made a distorted version of adab and allowed it to settle into our consciousness. A version that reduces character entirely to a style of communication. The peel.

To many, good manners now mean soft speech, frequent affirmations, and pleasant phrasing. The warmer the voice, the more adab is assumed to be present.

But to the Salaf, adab was far deeper. It wasn't a style; it was truthfulness, fairness, accuracy, and placing matters exactly where they belong.

The softness of speech and the reality of adab are not the same thing. One does not guarantee the other.

A person can speak gently while being insincere.

A person can speak warmly while being manipulative.

A person can speak pleasantly while being unjust.

And a person can speak plainly while possessing excellent character.

The distortion of adab then leads to a distortion elsewhere:

When verbal warmth becomes the standard, two things happen:

An entire category of sincere people gets misread and wronged.

An entire category of insincere people gets passed off as virtuous.

Injustice wrapped in social niceties.

― ✦ ―

## Temperament & Character

One reason this happens is because many people unconsciously treat their preferred temperament as the Islamic standard.

People who perform closeness easily have usually never had to question it. Nothing objected to it, so it settled into the shape of the truth.

So when they meet someone who does not operate that way, they do not think different. They think deficient. They do not observe — they diagnose.

The reserved individual becomes a case study. Something to be fixed.

Confusing concepts leads to a flawed perception of reality.

Because temperament describes how a person naturally expresses himself.

Character describes whether he is truthful, sincere, trustworthy, fair, loyal, and beneficial.

These categories overlap at times, but they are not identical.

A warm person can possess poor character.

A reserved person can possess excellent character.

An expressive person can be unreliable.

A quiet person can be deeply loyal.

The Companions of the Prophet ﷺ were not a single temperamental type.

ʿUmar bnu al-Khaṭṭāb was known for his directness, his sharpness, and his formidable presence.

Abū Bakr was quieter and softer.

ʿUthmān was famously shy.

Abū Tharr was frank in his disposition.

Each was distinct, and the Prophet ﷺ did not instruct any of them to converge on a single emotional style.

The Prophetic model does not demand temperamental uniformity. It demands character. It demands truthfulness, justice, loyalty, and the fulfillment of rights.

Human temperaments differ. Character standards do not.

― ✦ ―

## Selective Expression

Once warmth is mistaken for the substance of adab, it follows naturally that visibility becomes the sole metric of character. If it is noticeable, you are mannered; if it dips, you lack manners.

To be considered virtuous within this framework, one must remain in a state of constant performance.

Let’s look at another example where the salaf demonstrated immense social intelligence, awareness, and real bonding.

ʿUmar bnu al-Khaṭṭāb once sought entry to see the Prophet ﷺ. He asked a servant to seek permission for him. The servant went in, spoke to the Prophet ﷺ, and returned to inform ʿUmar that the Prophet ﷺ had remained silent.

ʿUmar did not interpret this silence as an insult or a personal rejection; he simply withdrew and sat near the pulpit.

Driven by concern, he returned a second time, only for the exact same silence to occur. He sat down again. Only on his third attempt was he granted entry.

Upon entering, ʿUmar found the Prophet ﷺ lying on a bare reed mat that left deep impressions on his side, leaning against a leather pillow stuffed with harsh palm fiber.

ʿUmar greeted him and, while still standing, asked a direct question about whether he had divorced his wives. The Prophet ﷺ raised his eyes and answered with a single word: "No".

This exchange reveals something far deeper than a fatigued leader being brief. It demonstrates that the established reality of their bond was doing the heavy lifting, completely independent of verbal validation.

ʿUmar was turned away twice without receiving it as a slight or a sign of fractured brotherhood.

When he finally received that one-word answer, he did not treat the brevity as a deficiency or demand an artificial display of warmth from the Prophet ﷺ.

Instead, he read the state of the room, calibrated his approach with light conversation to ease the tension, and waited for the warmth to emerge naturally and unforced.

Prophetic adab, therefore, is not a generalized emotional warmth broadcast uniformly in all directions.

Selective, considered, and grounded expression is inherently closer to this standard than perpetual performative enthusiasm.

Consistency of performance is not the Islamic standard. Sincerity of reality is.

― ✦ ―

## Distance, Closeness,  
and the Salaf

Al-imām al-Khaṭṭābī, in his book al-ʿUzlah (Seclusion), preserves a series of accounts that reframe the entire modern assumption about what closeness between brothers should look like.

Shabīb bnu Shayba said: "Among my brothers are those who visit me only once a year — these are the ones I take and count upon in life and death. And among them are those who visit me every day, who kiss me and whom I kiss — and if I could replace my kiss with a bite, I would bite them".

A full year of physical absence, and yet they remained true brothers.

Daily meetings, but nothing concrete.

The frequency of contact was entirely decoupled from the actual weight of the bond.

ʿAbdu ar-Raḥmān bnu Mahdī and Yaḥyā bnu Saʿīd al-Qaṭṭān were known for their deep bond. Yet a year would frequently pass without a single face-to-face meeting between them. When one was asked about this, he said: "When hearts are close, physical distance does no harm".

The poet says:

I saw two intimate friends parting in virtue  
when hearts had settled upon sincere affection.  
Only the one suspicious of his own love  
keeps visiting constantly. 

This rigorous standard runs consistently through the heritage of the early generations.

Bishr bnu al-Ḥārith said: "The most beloved of my brothers to me is the one who neither sees me nor do I see him".

And when Sufyān ath-Thawrī was asked to point out a man whose companionship was genuinely worth seeking, he replied with heavy realism: "That is something that cannot be found".

These are not the words of cold or indifferent men. These are the words of men who understood that the reality of brotherhood resides securely in the heart and manifests in concrete action when it truly matters—not in the superficial frequency of visits, modern check-ins, or verbal declarations.

The poet said:

My brother is not the one who befriends me with his tongue  
but the one who stands with me in calamities.  
Whose wealth is mine when I am destitute,  
and mine is his when time presses hard upon him.  
Do not praise a companion during ease  
for brothers often turn away when disasters come. 

― ✦ ―

## Emotional Seriousness

The divergence in how people communicate is not merely a difference in style; it is a difference in how they view the weight of language itself.

Some speak freely because words come easily to them. Others speak cautiously because words carry the weight of a debt.

Praise is a testimony.

Love is a serious judgment.

Loyalty is a lifelong commitment.

Affection is a heavy responsibility.

Because these concepts carry such immense gravity, these individuals hesitate to spend large words on small realities.

They refuse to use the vocabulary of deep spiritual bonds to describe casual, unearned interactions. This scarcity of expression is never evidence of emotional poverty; it is the manifestation of emotional seriousness.

The friction arises because the highly expressive type rarely understands that this second way of being exists. Because their own emotional style feels natural and universally correct, they have quietly mistaken performative warmth for the baseline of human decency.

They do not realize that someone else might be experiencing a reality too deep to easily verbalize.

― ✦ ―

## What Brotherhood Actually Requires

The word "brotherhood" is deployed constantly in contemporary spaces, completely detached from its weight.

What it has become, in reality, is a contract nobody signed: be present constantly, respond warmly, and perform closeness visibly enough that the other person feels reassured.

If that warmth ever dips, and even for reasons completely unrelated to the bond itself—something is automatically assumed to be wrong. With you.

True brotherhood requires no such maintenance. When the foundation is solid, distance is merely a circumstance—not a threat.

A real bond depends on what a person is actually made of, not on how well they perform when they are right in front of you.

A person can perform social warmth flawlessly, mastering the external posture of intimacy, while possessing absolutely no capacity for a deeper bond.

A person can be reserved, even terse, and remain entirely unshakeable when the weight of life demands a true ally.

The man who stands by you in hardship, who shields your honor in you absence, who tells you the uncomfortable truth when you need to hear it—this is a brother, irrespective of his verbal output.

Brotherhood is not a matter of shared emotional styles. It is what remains when the warmth of words is tested by the coldness of circumstance.

― ✦ ―

## The penalty of prematurity

مَنْ تَعَجَّلَ شَيْئًا قَبْلَ أَوَانِهِ عُوقِبَ بِحِرْمَانِهِ.

Whoever rushes a thing before its proper time is punished by being deprived of it.

The jurists (al-fuqahāʾ) established this profound maxim regarding human actions and their consequences.

A principle which the modern demand for instant intimacy continually violates.

True connection, mutual trust, and spiritual love are fruits that grow on a specific, unalterable timeline. They require the slow accumulation of shared trials, proven consistency, and time.

When people attempt to bypass this process—using the heaviest, most sacred vocabulary of brotherhood to force an intimacy that has not yet been forged—they trigger the exact spiritual penalty described by the jurists.

By demanding the immediate rewards of deep companionship before its proper time, we are punished with its ultimate deprivation.

We receive the performative wrapper, but lose the substance.

We are left with an exhausting loop of mutual reassurance, always sensing underneath it that the foundation cannot bear the weight of the words.

Seen in this light, the reserved stance is not an emotional deficiency or a social awkwardness awaiting correction. It is a quiet, disciplined adherence to the law of timing.

The refusal to broadcast unearned warmth is an act of preservation. It recognizes that if a profound bond is ever to exist between two people, it cannot be conjured through premature declarations. It must be allowed its proper time, protected by a measured silence until the reality catches up to the language.

― ✦ ―

## Conclusion

Words are not realities; they are merely signs pointing toward them.

The one who understands this, refuses to speak until the word is true, they reserve expression for what has been earned, and allow silence to carry what speech cannot.

They honor the true weight of a testimony.

But, modern sensibilities look at these people and diagnose them as cold, which says everything about the superficiality of contemporary social culture, and nothing about their character.

This goes far deeper than a few cheapened phrases. We have built a culture where knowing the vocabulary is routinely mistaken for mastering the framework.

And, in a time when language has never been cheaper, those who guard their words are not a problem to be corrected.

They are a rare and necessary reminder of what sincerity actually looks like.

We are the problem, as we failed to recognize them.

هذا، والله أعلم.  
وصلى الله على نبينا محمد.
