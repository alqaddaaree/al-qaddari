Al-Halabi: Our Shaykh, a questioner asks about the athkaar (supplications) after prayer. Should they be said aloud? Some well-known shuyukh have said that abandoning loud thikr (supplicating) post prayer is one of the neglected Sunnahs.

He also mentioned the hadeeth of Ibn-Abbaas in Saheeh al-Bukhaari—that the Prophet — peace and belessings be upon him — used to recite aloud and the Companions did as well.

What is your opinion?

  
The shaykh:  
The hadeeth referred to is authentic, no doubt, narrated by Imaam al-Bukhaari in his Saheeh. But it is not a text proving the continuity of making thikr aloud after prayer, nor does it indicate that all types of thikr legislated after prayer are meant to be done aloud.  
  
As for the first point: the hadeeth does not indicate that the Prophet — peace and belessings be upon him — and the Companions always continued raising their voices after prayer. This is clear based on two things:  
  
1\. The wording of the hadeeth itself, from the narrator Ibn-Abbaas (may Allah be pleased with him): “We used to know the end of the Prophet’s prayer by the raising of the voice with thikr.” His saying “we used to” shows it did not continue permanently.  
  
  
For this reason, Imaam ash-Shafi‘ee said in his great book al-Umm that this loud thikr was for the purpose of teaching. The Prophet — peace and belessings be upon him — would sometimes raise his voice in thikr to teach some athkaar after the prayer, just as we know he would sometimes raise his voice in reciting Qur’aan, even in silent prayers, so the Companions would know what he was reciting.

Ibn-Abbaas himself said: “We used to know the Prophet’s recitation in silent prayers by the movement of his beard". That doesn’t tell them what exactly he recited. But Abu-Qataadah al-Ansaaree said: “He would sometimes let us hear a verse". So if he raised his voice in places where normally it is not legislated, only to teach, then all the more reason he would raise it after the prayer for teaching, though the norm for thikr after prayer is that it is recited quietly.  
  
There are many narrations encouraging not to raise voices in thikr—even outdoors in the desert.

In the two Saheehs, Abo Moosa al-Ash‘ari narrated: “We were traveling with the Prophet — peace and belessings be upon him —. When we ascended a small hill we would say takbeer, and when we descended a valley we would say tasbeeh, raising our voices. The Prophet — peace and belessings be upon him — said: ‘O people, be gentle with yourselves. You are not calling upon one who is deaf or absent, rather you are calling upon One who is All-Hearing, All-Seeing, closer to each of you than the neck of his ride'".  
  
If that applies in the open desert, then in the masjid even more so, because loud thikr disturbs latecomers completing their prayer, or those reciting Qur’aan, or making their own athkaar.

The Prophet — peace and belessings be upon him — once heard people raising their voices in thikr in the masjid and said: “Each of you is privately conversing with his Lord, so do not raise your voices over one another in recitation.” In another wording: “…in Qur’an.”  
  
So there is no proof in the hadeeth of Ibn-Abbaas for continuity of loud thikr.  
  
As for the second point, that the hadeeth doesn’t show loud thikr applies to all athkar after prayer—this is even clearer. I don’t think those shuyukh who said that raising the voice in thikr is from the neglected Sunnahs actually mean that one should raise his voice with every dhikr. Nobody says, for example, that one group says Subhanallah Subhanallah aloud here and another says it there. No scholar holds that view.  
  
At most, there are specific adhkar where it’s reported that the Prophet ﷺ raised his voice, and the reason, as said, was to teach. As for other supplications, such as “Allahumma a‘inni ‘ala thikrika wa shukrika wa husni ‘ibaadatik” which the Prophet ﷺ recommended to Mu‘adh ibn Jabal after every prayer, this is said quietly. Likewise “Allahumma anta as-Salaam wa minka as-salaam…” and similar. These are not recited aloud.  
  
What is recited aloud? The ten tahlilaat (saying Laa ilaaha illa Allah ten times) after Fajr and after Maghrib. Why these specifically and not the others? There is no clear distinction.  
  
So, to conclude: the hadeeth contains no explicit proof of continuity and no proof of generality to all athkaar. That is the end of the answer.
