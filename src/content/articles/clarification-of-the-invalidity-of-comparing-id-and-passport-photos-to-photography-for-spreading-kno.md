In the name of Allah, and may blessings and peace be upon the Messenger of Allah. To proceed:  
  
This is a response from sheiykh Najeeb ash-Shar'abi – may Allah preserve him – to Dr. Saleem al-Hilaalee concerning the permissibility of photographic imaging for the purpose of spreading knowledge, and the faulty analogy used to justify it.  
  
We will summarize the meaning of the sheikh’s reply from the machine transcription of the audio, and pick its core points.  
  
Dr. Saleem said:  


> This is a fatwa from our Shaykh Ibn Baaz – may Allah have mercy on him – permitting video and television recording for the broadcasting of lessons in knowledge and da‘wah to Allah, and that people are in greater need of this than of an ID photo, passport, or driver’s license. And that the issue is fiqh-related, not aqeedah-related; it does not warrant tabdi‘, nor boycotting, nor anything of the sort. It should not divide the students of knowledge or distract them from the duty of the time in knowleldge, action, and da‘wah. May Allah guide everyone to good and unite us upon Islam and the Sunnah.

The sheikh was asked: what is your opinion on this matter?

And the meaning of his answer is as follows:

Discussing the fatwa of Imaam Ibn Baaz used as evidence:  
  
Ibn Baaz said in the fatwa referenced by sheikh Saleem al-Hilaalee:

I had previously been hesitant concerning images, this television which transmits images, pictures of lectures and conferences, intended for good purposes. I used to be uneasy about that and would not allow it during the lecture itself. Then it appeared to me that I should permit it for the general benefit of the Muslims, so they may benefit from the conference or the lecturer, since its benefit reaches more widely. So if taking a photograph for an ID and the like, which is an individual benefit and need, is allowed, then how much more so for needs and benefits that are widespread? This is what strengthened my view that there is no harm in recording lectures and conferences.  
  
* **The rebuttal to this reasoning:** What must be followed and submitted to is the evidence from the Qur’an and Sunnah. The opposition of any great figure does not nullify the evidences of the Qur’an and Sunnah, even if the one opposing is of great stature.  
  
* **Ibn Baz’s earlier stance was stricter:** Imam Ibn Baz, may Allah have mercy on him, previously prohibited video recording of living beings by the general evidences, except in cases of necessity or coercion. He exempted only what necessity required, such as photographing criminals, medical issues, or for identification (license, ID card, passport), cases resembling compulsion.  
  
* **The analogy is invalid:** He later allowed video recording of lectures and conferences, arguing it was a public benefit greater than an individual need. But on what evidence did Imam Ibn Baz base this? None. He relied on an invalid analogy: comparing recording lectures to ID photos and licenses. This is a faulty comparison, since the ID photo is under compulsion and necessity, while recording a lecture – is there compulsion? is there necessity? No.  
  
* **Alternative to recording images:** There is no necessity to film lectures and conferences in order to spread them and benefit people. They can be recorded and broadcast with sound alone, achieving the intended benefit. The image adds no knowledge; the goal is the audio.  
  
**Practical evidence that there is no necessity:**  
  
* The immense benefits Allah brought through the two Imams Ibn Baz and Ibn ‘Uthaymin spread without videos, but rather through audio fatwas and writings.  
  
* Imam al-Albani, may Allah have mercy on him, had hundreds of tapes in his series *Silsilat al-Huda wa al-Nur* and books that filled the earth, benefitting the Ummah tremendously, all without images, and he would thunder against the prohibition of photography.  
  
* Imam al-Wadi‘i, may Allah have mercy on him, benefitted through his students and writings. Were these through videos? No. He too used to thunder against photography.  
  
* Our Shaykh Yahya – may Allah preserve him – has no equal in our time among the people of knowledge in terms of benefit, students, and the spread of good. All this spread widely without videos.  
  
The matter is clear, my brother. The Salafi da‘wah has benefitted the Ummah immensely without images or videos.

I said: May Allah reward the Shaykh for this clarification which no fair seeker of truth can deny.  
  
And to reinforce the benefit, the key point in the reply is:  
  
* **The analogy is invalid:** Ibn Baz later permitted recording lectures and conferences, arguing that public benefit outweighs individual need. But what evidence did he cite? None. He relied on an invalid analogy: comparing the videoing of lectures with ID photos and licenses. This is a false analogy, because ID photos are under compulsion and necessity, whereas lectures are neither compelled nor necessary.  
  
I said: There is in fact no need at all. We have no need for a Shaykh’s picture; this is among the strangest things I have heard on this issue. They claim there is benefit in recording images, but they never specify what that benefit is. Of course not, because there is none. Rather, it is sheer corruption and grave evil. Allah has not made the cure of this Ummah in what He has forbidden for it. The cure for ignorance is knowledge, not images. Allah’s help is sought.  
  
The Messenger of Allah – peace and blessings be upon him – did not receive this religion from our Lord through images. The Companions did not receive from the Messenger of Allah through images. The scholars of Ahl al-Sunnah did not inherit this religion through images. We do not know the form of the golden calf worshipped by the Jews in the Qur’an, nor the body motions in the chapters of prayer, nor portraits of the men whose biographies were recorded.  
  
And their saying: *“This issue should not divide the students of knowledge and distract them”* is false and slander. The one who trivializes a major sin is the divider, not the one who forbids it. Whoever has not had his insight blinded from recognizing the gravity of this evil will indeed forbid it and be severe about it, since the matter touches upon the sanctity of tawhid. It is not, as they claim, a mere fiqh issue. Glory be to my Lord, how has the trivialization of major sins without necessity become “fiqh”?  
  
Shaykh Rabi‘ al-Madkhali, may Allah have mercy on him, even pointed out that the da‘wah contracted after the spread of photography, and he knows its state best.  
  
In the end, the only proof relied upon is an invalid analogy. From there, people expanded until their pages are filled with photographs for the most trivial of reasons, all under the pretense of Ibn Baz’s fatwa. But in reality, his practical conduct was clearer than that lone fatwa, for his lessons and rulings were spread without images, and he strongly warned against those who spread his pictures.  
  
Reality now falls into three categories:  
  
1\. A follower of his desires, turning away from the truth.  
2\. One deficient in understanding and awareness, failing to grasp the danger of trivializing such a grave sin.  
3\. One captive to the prevalence of images and afraid that people would be turned away from his da‘wah – and this is evident weakness.  
  
Allah’s help is sought, and to Him is the complaint.
