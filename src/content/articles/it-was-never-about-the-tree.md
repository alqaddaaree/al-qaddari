You tasted the fruit.

You liked it.

You said so.

You came back for more.

* * *

At first, you cared about one thing.

The fruit.

Was it useful?  
Did it help?  
Did it nourish?

Yes.

That was enough.

* * *

Then something changed.

Not in the fruit.

In your gaze.

* * *

You started looking at the tree.

Not for shade.  
Not for fruit.

For faults.

* * *

"This branch is crooked."

"The trunk is rough."

"This isn't the kind of tree I expected."

* * *

Yet the fruit… stayed the same.

Still useful.  
Still beneficial.

But now, you were no longer focused on eating.

You were focused on judging.

* * *

You kept tasting.

But between bites, complaints started growing.

Small at first.

Almost harmless.

Then louder.

More frequent.

Until one day…

you stopped eating altogether.

Not because the fruit failed you.

Because you convinced yourself the tree was the problem.

 

############################

 

Good. This next section is the hinge. The quiet click inside the lock. If you get this part right, the rest almost writes itself.

The key is: **nothing dramatic happens outwardly.**  
Everything happens inside first.

* * *

You didn’t stop eating right away.

That’s the strange part.

You still came back.  
Still tasted.  
Still benefited.

But something new had entered the room.

Comparison.

Not loud. Not announced.

Just… there.

* * *

You noticed others gathering around the tree.

Taking fruit.

Speaking well of it.

Returning often.

And suddenly, the fruit was no longer just fruit.

It became… connected to the tree.

And the tree became… something you measured yourself against.

* * *

Before, you asked:

"Is this useful?"

Now, a different question slipped in:

"Why _this_ tree?"

* * *

That question sounds harmless.

Almost intelligent.

But it carries heat inside it.

Because it isn’t really about the fruit anymore.

It’s about position.  
About standing.  
About who gives… and who takes.

* * *

And here, something subtle begins to form.

Not rejection.

Not yet.

**Suspicion.**

You start watching the tree differently.

Not looking for fruit.

Looking for imperfections.

* * *

A rough patch on the bark.

A branch that grew uneven.

A leaf that doesn't match the rest.

And once you begin looking this way…

you will always find something.

Every tree has rough bark.

Every branch grows imperfectly.

* * *

This is the moment most people miss.

The turning point isn’t when they walk away.

It’s when they **start collecting complaints while still benefiting.**

That’s when the heart quietly changes posture.

Not outwardly. Inwardly.

* * *

And from there, something predictable happens.

The mind begins to assist the feeling.

It gathers reasons.  
Builds arguments.  
Finds language to dress the irritation.

What began as discomfort becomes "principle."

What began as comparison becomes "discernment."

And now, the person feels justified.

Even wise.

While the benefit… slowly weakens.

* * *

There’s a harsh honesty in this stage.

Because the fruit is still good.

Still working.

Still helping.

But now every bite tastes slightly bitter.

Not because of the fruit.

Because of what grew inside the eater.

That’s **зависть (zavist')** at work in its early form. Not explosive. Not obvious. Just quietly reshaping perception until benefit starts feeling uncomfortable instead of welcome.
