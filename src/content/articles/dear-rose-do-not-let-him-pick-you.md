At first, it feels clean.

Actually, cleaner than I expected.

I always thought these things would be obvious.

A man who is not serious would sound unserious.

A man who wants to play would speak like someone playing.

But he is not like that.

He is careful with his words.

He does not say filthy things.

He does not push.

He does not make me feel cheap.

He asks about marriage.

He mentions my walī.

He says he wants things done properly.

That matters.

A careless man would not say that.

A man playing games would not speak about responsibility.

So when he asks how I am, I answer.

Why would I not?

It is just a question.

When he remembers something I said, I notice.

Of course I notice.

People do not usually pay attention like that.

When he says he was thinking about what I mentioned yesterday, something in me softens.

Not in a shameful way.

Just… in a human way.

It is nice to be heard.

It is nice to be spoken to gently.

It is nice when someone sees something in you and does not treat it carelessly.

And I am not foolish.

I know the limits.

I know where the line is.

We are not doing what other people do.

There is no flirting like that.

No vulgar speech.

No secret promises.

No nonsense.

Only careful words.

Only small messages.

Only enough to understand each other.

How else is a person supposed to know?

People say everything must go through the walī, and yes, of course.

But surely there has to be some understanding first.

Surely a man does not go to a family knowing nothing.

Surely a woman is allowed to know whether someone is serious.

And he does seem serious.

He says soon.

He says he is arranging things.

He says he wants the proper way.

Maybe he is just waiting for the right moment.

Maybe his situation is not easy.

Maybe I should be patient.

I would not want to be unfair.

And anyway, nothing has happened.

I am still myself.

I still pray.

I still know right from wrong.

I still have control over my heart.

I am not like the girls who get carried away.

I can stop if I need to.

I can keep things balanced.

It is only that my day feels a little different now.

When he writes, I feel lighter.

When he delays, I wonder why.

When something happens, I think of telling him.

When I read something beautiful, I wonder what he would say.

But that is normal, is it not?

When someone might become part of your life, of course you begin to make space for him in your thoughts.

That is not wrong by itself.

That is just how the heart prepares.

And perhaps this is how it starts for everyone.

A little attention.

A little hope.

A little fear.

A little waiting.

A flower does not know the moment it begins to lean toward the hand.

It only knows that the hand is warm.

That it returns.

That it does not crush.

That it seems to admire without taking.

So it remains still.

And when the fingers touch the petals, it does not pull away.

Why would it?

The touch is gentle.

The voice is gentle.

The intention seems gentle.

And the garden is still around it.

Nothing has been picked.

Not yet.

But then the hand returns again.

And again.

At first, that feels like seriousness.

A man who keeps returning must mean what he says.

A man who remembers must care.

A man who asks must be thinking ahead.

So I make room for him.

Not too much.

Only enough to be fair.

Only enough to understand.

Only enough to see where this might go.

Then my phone becomes part of the waiting.

Not always.

Not in a desperate way.

But I notice.

I notice when he writes earlier than usual.

I notice when he writes later.

I notice when his tone changes.

I notice when he says less.

I notice when he says more.

And I tell myself this is normal.

How can something serious move forward without some concern?

How can two people consider marriage while feeling nothing?

I am not attached.

I am only paying attention.

There is a difference.

But the difference becomes harder to explain.

When he is warm, I feel settled.

When he is distant, I feel restless.

When he mentions speaking properly, I feel relieved.

When another week passes, I tell myself there must be a reason.

And there is always a reason.

His family.

His work.

His money.

His uncertainty.

The timing.

The need to prepare.

The need to avoid rushing.

The need to make sure.

All of it sounds responsible.

All of it sounds careful.

And because I do not want to be immature, I accept the waiting.

I would hate to be one of those girls who pressures a man.

I would hate to ruin something good by being impatient.

So I become patient.

But my patience is no longer empty.

It has his name inside it.

I do not tell anyone.

Not because I am hiding something.

There is nothing to hide.

It is only too early.

Too unfinished.

Too delicate.

People misunderstand matters when they see them before they are complete.

If I mention him now, they will ask questions I cannot answer yet.

Has he spoken to your walī?

When will he come?

Who is his family?

What exactly does he want?

And I do not want to turn something still forming into a public matter.

So I keep it quiet.

For now.

Until there is something real to say.

And anyway, he does mention the proper way.

He does not deny it.

He does not mock it.

He does not say my walī does not matter.

He only says:

Soon.

When things are clearer.

When the moment is right.

And I believe him.

Or maybe I believe the version of him that has been living in my mind.

It is difficult to tell when hope starts completing a person’s sentences for him.

The flower is still in the garden.

That is what makes it easy to remain calm.

The stem has not been cut.

The roots have not been pulled out.

No one has carried it away.

But the soil around it has loosened.

Quietly.

Each visit moves something.

Each touch takes a little firmness from the ground.

Each delay teaches the flower to wait upward, toward the hand, instead of downward, into the root.

And because the petals are still bright, nobody notices.

Even the flower does not notice.

It thinks:

I am still here.

I am still safe.

I am still myself.

But its life has begun to gather around something outside the soil.

Then one day, the hand does not return the same way.

Not cruelly.

That would almost be easier.

Cruelty gives the heart something clear to reject.

But this is softer.

A shorter reply.

A delayed answer.

A new difficulty.

A sentence that begins with:

I have been thinking.

Or:

My situation has changed.

Or:

I do not want to hurt you.

And suddenly all the waiting gathers in one place.

All the small excuses.

All the defended silences.

All the times I told myself soon.

All the ways I made room for him before he made a home for me.

The garden is still there.

My family is still there.

My walī is still there.

The proper door was always there.

But my heart had already been standing at another entrance.

A private one.

A quiet one.

One nobody forced open.

One I kept unlocked because I thought he was different.

And now I understand something I did not want to understand.

A flower does not have to be fully picked in one motion.

It can be loosened first.

Visited first.

Admired first.

Bent toward the hand first.

Made to live upward first.

Then, when it is finally taken, it almost feels natural.

And when it is left, it does not know how to stand as before.
